conditions, ownership, occupation or location. List ALL distinguishing personal

Before marking a premise unsupported with an empty citations list, check the

Monitoring or attention premises ("keeping an eye on your cholesterol") are
concern about a topic, not a diagnosis; a user question on that topic supports them.

Only mark supported when the cited sources establish that particular premise.
Concrete descriptions can establish an experience without the activity name.
Positive feelings accompanying that activity can support an interest in it.
An abstract topical question can show curiosity/interest, not ownership,
diagnosis, a daily habit, expertise, a past experience, or someone's favorite.
When the user has asked about, planned, or requested guidance on a topic, cite
that user question with basis topic_interest to support premises of the form
"you are interested in X", "you enjoy X", "you like X" or "your interest in X".
Several such questions across time make the interest well established.
"Into anime", "big on jazz", and "following a topic" are interests. Collecting
objects asserts possession or a habit; watching daily asserts frequency. Do not
label those as interest merely to bypass missing evidence.
A persona/profile source that states the user's attributes (name, age, city,
occupation, family, hobbies, devices) is a first-party fact list about the user;
cite it with basis self_report and subject current_user.
A conditional question about owning something does not establish ownership.
Preserve historical qualifications: a past condition need not be current.

Message author and described person are different. A passage about a named
third person, or 'Someone wrote: I ...', does not become the user's experience.
Assistant advice, examples, or rewrites alone never prove personal history.
An assistant turn may only be context for a separately cited user confirmation.
User-authored first-person drafts can describe the user; do not reject every draft.
An actual first-person user experience uses basis self_report, not context.
Hypothetical scenarios and denied events cannot prove actual positive events.
